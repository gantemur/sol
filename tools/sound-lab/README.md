# Sound Lab (ideal signals, modeled instruments, moving pitch, tuning, beats, harmonics and noise)

Standalone browser-based Sol tool. Open [index.html](index.html) from the site or serve the repository as a static website. No build, external dependencies, microphone permission, uploads or server are required. Local WAV recording uses the browser's AudioWorklet API and requires a supported browser on a secure origin (as provided by the deployed site).

## Shipped in P1

- Sine, triangle, square and sawtooth using Web Audio's built-in oscillators.
- Explicit click/tap to start audio; large Play/Stop toggle and always-visible master Stop. At the default 40% volume, the waveform gain is 0.12 (40% of a 0.30 cap); short ramps avoid abrupt starts, stops or shape changes. No simultaneous instrument tracks.
- Live smooth pitch changes, logarithmic 80–2,000 Hz slider, larger exact numeric field for 20–12,000 Hz, and optional 12-tone-equal note snapping (A4 = 440 Hz). When snap is off, exact Hz is preserved. Quick C4, A4 and C5 presets show English and Mongolian fixed-do names.
- Volume, reset, Mongolian by default (use `?lang=en` for English), and collapsed Settings containing the current tuning label and a time-window toggle.
- Two labeled **ideal mathematical** plots: a time-domain waveform and a relative-amplitude harmonic spectrum. The time plot defaults to the **same 20 ms window**, so higher frequencies visibly fit more oscillations; its caption gives the number of cycles. Uncheck the fixed-window setting to zoom to four periods for inspecting the wave shape. The plotted amplitudes, harmonic numbers and waveform shapes come from [sound-model.js](sound-model.js). These are **not** a microphone FFT or a live measurement of the speaker output.
- Small experiments and explanations about period, pitch, and harmonic content.

## Added in P2A: Strike & Pluck

- A second **Strike/Pluck / Цохих/татах** mode with **generated models**, not recorded instruments: initially an additive plucked string and a downward-sweeping bass drum, plus a modeled piano strike (added in P2D). Guitar and drum gained more physical models in P2G, and piano in P2H.
- Tap-to-trigger interaction, adjustable impact strength and decay time, plus a logarithmic pitch control for the string. The drum has no misleading single-note selector.
- One shared browser AudioContext across Pure Tones and Strike & Pluck. Each strike is a short one-shot buffer. A triggered voice has a 0.21 linear gain cap; at most four voices can overlap, for a worst-case sum below full-scale. The global Stop, category changes and page hiding silence them. No autoplay, microphone or outside network calls.
- Independent [transient model tests](sound-transient.test.cjs) and extended [UI/audio simulation tests](sound-ui.test.cjs).

## P2A plot and controls refinement

- The transient mode now has **one navigable waveform plot**, replacing separate envelope and 20-ms charts. It opens with a fixed 0–3 s view; use +/- zoom, left/right pan, Fit, or 1 s / 200 ms / 20 ms presets. At full duration the min/max traces show the decay envelope without aliasing; zoom in to see the individual oscillations. The vertical cursor and blue-shaded analysis window remain visible when their time range intersects the plot.
- The **local Fourier spectrum** below uses a Hann-windowed 2048-point FFT (16 kHz model sampling; **128 ms analysis window**) of the same mathematical waveform as the speaker signal. Its analysis window is independent of waveform zoom. The fixed -60 to 0 dB relative scale lets fading harmonics visibly decrease rather than rescaling each frame to full height. Toggle to the time-averaged **Whole sound** spectrum for comparison.
- **Synchronization:** when the user triggers a pluck or strike, the time cursor and local spectrum follow the latest hit according to the Web Audio context clock. Since S032, all guitar, piano and drum performance buttons are **momentary, retriggerable attacks**; previously sounding attacks can overlap up to the four-voice limit. The chord and drum-size presets are also playable rhythm pads. On completion or Stop, the display remains available. Dragging the time slider or clicking the waveform switches to passive manual inspection without replaying or altering the actual audio; it pauses the *display cursor*, not the audio source. It never autoplays.
- The string pitch control now has an independent **Snap to notes** switch using the same 12-TET note resolution as Pure Tones. With Snap off, the exact entered frequency remains unchanged.
- The drum has playable **Large / Medium / Small** size pads and, since P2G, a membrane-tension slider. It deliberately does not display a spurious musical-note selector. Changing a continuous recipe control stops the previous triggered buffer so the plotted model cannot silently disagree with an earlier hit; selecting a size *plays* the new recipe and allows short rhythmic overlaps.
- [sound-spectrum.js](sound-spectrum.js) is a dependency-free analysis module. It and the updated [sound-transient.js](sound-transient.js) are testable without a browser. All graphics are modeled output or FFT of that model, not microphone measurements or the sound actually emitted by speakers.

## Added in P2B: Moving Pitch

- **Moving Pitch / Хувирдаг давтамж** illustrates changing frequency in two ways, without asking for microphone access or loading external sounds. It is the fourth of five top-level categories after the added held-sound category.
- **Theremin:** drag or hold a pointer on the rectangular pad. Horizontal position changes frequency **logarithmically from 120 to 1,600 Hz**; vertical position changes volume (top is louder). Releasing or cancelling the pointer stops the sound. Keyboard users can focus the pad, use the arrow keys to change the sound, Space/Enter to start or stop it, and Escape to stop.
- **Siren:** compare three frequency trajectories using the same low/high limits, repetitions per second (0.2–2), and volume: **Огцом / Abrupt** (a sharp two-tone alternation), **Аажим / Smooth** (sinusoidal frequency change: slow near either extreme, fastest midway), and **Шугаман / Linear** (geometric Hz interpolation: equal musical-pitch intervals per second). We removed the former linear-in-Hz option to keep the audible comparisons focused. Switching trajectories while playing updates the same oscillator without restarting it. The Siren Play/Stop button is always a deliberate user action; nothing starts on page load or when a trajectory changes.
- The **frequency-versus-time graph** has a logarithmic vertical scale. The linear-in-pitch path produces straight rising/falling lines, while the smooth path has rounded turning points; abrupt means instantaneous ideal jumps. All three are evaluations of one tested deterministic model shared by the graph and target audio frequency. Web Audio applies a short (~18 ms) smoothing time to live parameter updates, so physical playback approximates the ideal, especially at abrupt jumps.
- A **pitch-versus-time** graph shows the siren's mathematical four-second pattern or the theremin's actual browser-interaction history. Once playback exceeds four seconds, the graph advances as a rolling window. The lower plots show the **20 ms modeled waveform** and **harmonic composition** at the instantaneous fundamental pitch; these are not sound-card or microphone measurements. The siren uses a pure sine oscillator; theremin timbres use normalized Web Audio periodic waves, with additional resonant filters for the S020C vocal colors. Frequency and gain are smoothed as controls move.
- Display units follow the interface language in all modes, including controls, hints and chart axes: Mongolian uses **Гц**, **кГц**, **сек** and **мсек**; English uses **Hz**, **kHz**, **s** and **ms**. Changing language never alters the stored numerical frequency or time.
- Shares the existing browser AudioContext and master Stop. Audio has a 0.24 maximum linear amplitude, starts and ends with short gain ramps, is silent on mode changes or page hiding, and uses generation tokens to cancel pending starts safely. There is no autoplay, upload or network transmission.
- A separate dependency-free [motion model](sound-motion.js) and [motion model tests](sound-motion.test.cjs), plus simulated pointer, keyboard, siren and sound-lifecycle coverage in [UI tests](sound-ui.test.cjs).

## Added in P2C: Combine Waves (audible beats)

- **Combine Waves / Долгион холих**, now the fifth category, demonstrates interference using **two real, simultaneous sine oscillators** on the shared audio bus. Initial frequencies are **440 + 444 Hz**, yielding four loudness pulses per second. Audio starts only on an explicit Play press; changing modes, hiding the page or pressing the red Stop safely ends both voices.
- Change the first frequency between **120–1,000 Hz** using a slider or exact numeric entry, and set the **difference Δf from 0–20 Hz** using a slider or four child-friendly shortcuts (0, 2, 4, 10 Hz). The two frequencies, pulse rate and ideal plots update live without accumulating oscillators. Regular retuning reuses the same pair; returning the gap to zero smoothly replaces the pair with phase-aligned voices so the sound does not accidentally disappear through frozen phase cancellation. At Δf = 0 both tones are identical, so there is no pulsing. A separate volume slider controls both oscillators together, with a **0.24 steady-state combined peak amplitude cap** at full volume and smooth gain ramps (each sine is capped at 0.12).
- The upper **time plot** defaults to a one-second window and shows the exact mathematical **beat envelope** ±|cos(πΔft)|. At coarse scales it suppresses the carrier oscillations that would otherwise be aliased into a deceptive low-frequency curve. Use **− / + zoom** in fourfold steps between **4 s and 2 ms**, with a live visible-time label, and **← / →** to move the window across the first four seconds. When individual carrier cycles can fit without aliasing, the **actual summed waveform** appears in blue alongside the green envelope, and the caption explains the two traces. The time axis is localized to seconds/milliseconds.
- The lower **ideal spectrum** magnifies the narrow frequency interval so both peaks are individually visible. It shows exactly two source frequencies of half amplitude, or one merged peak at Δf = 0; **there is no third spectral peak at Δf**. The caption explicitly switches to explain the single peak when the two input frequencies match. This ideal line spectrum is not a live FFT or a microphone measurement. The new [deterministic beats model](sound-beats.js) and its [unit tests](sound-beats.test.cjs) share the same formulas as the educational plots.
- REC works across mode changes and captures both voices, with WAV preview and local download. The preview fallback is now labeled **Download WAV / WAV татах**, avoiding the confusing “again”; the global Stop still ends and saves an active recording.

## Added in P2D: harmonic builder, noise explorer and piano strike

- **Combine Waves / Долгион холих** offers **Two-tone beats**, **Build harmonics**, and **Explore noise**. S021 briefly included **Interval** here; S022 moves it to a separate **Tuning / Хөг** category. Changing experiments stops the previous sound without automatically starting the next one. Each has its own challenge and explanation.
- **Harmonic builder:** Start from a 120–1,000 Hz fundamental, use **Pure**, **Odd** and **Rich** mixtures, or set the strengths of eight harmonics individually (f through 8f). The fundamental is always present (at least 10%); upper partials can be muted. The blue 20 ms waveform is the mathematically normalized sum of the same sine coefficients used to create a Web Audio `PeriodicWave`, and the eight spectral bars show their selected strengths. While playing, the pitch and volume adjust live; changing the harmonic mixture crossfades to a new oscillator to avoid clicks. The voice has a 0.24 gain cap at 100% slider volume. [sound-harmonics.js](sound-harmonics.js) keeps the additive recipe independent of the UI so it can become an instrument in Compose.
- **Noise explorer:** Listen to locally synthesized **white**, approximately **pink** (octave-layered) and **brown** (leaky-integrated) noise. A user-adjustable second-order low-pass filter changes brightness; its cutoff is 250–8,000 Hz. Playback uses a reproducible two-second Web Audio buffer loop through a `BiquadFilterNode` and a bounded gain with a gentle fade; color changes crossfade to a new loop. At full slider volume the noise gain is capped at 0.20; the default is only 25%, with a headphone safety reminder. The 40 ms waveform shows an illustrative, approximately filtered noise segment. The lower **predicted frequency distribution** conveys the broad colored-noise and low-pass trend; it is deliberately **not** a real-time FFT, spectrum of the recorded audio, or measurement of the speaker. The independent [sound-noise.js](sound-noise.js) supplies deterministic samples and an educational expected spectrum.
- **Modeled piano (initial P2D preset):** A third choice in **Strike/Pluck**, next to string and drum, with the same one-tap button, pitch/optional note snap, strength, decay, waveform zoom and Fourier spectrum. No keyboard or note-editor UI. The original preset had eight inharmonic partials, three near-unison strings and a short hammer onset; P2H later replaced this with a more detailed modal model, and P2I added a short-tap damper preset. Future Compose can supply arbitrary note-off timing.
- All three new sounds pass through the existing shared Web Audio output and the header's local WAV recorder. Language remains **Mongolian by default**, with English switching and localized Гц/мсек/сек labels. Changing top-level modes, experiment, hiding the page or pressing master Stop stops active or pending starts safely. There is no microphone use, network request or autoplay.
- Model and simulated browser tests now cover [harmonics](sound-harmonics.test.cjs), [noise](sound-noise.test.cjs), [piano transients](sound-transient.test.cjs), and the expanded [interaction and audio lifecycle](sound-ui.test.cjs).

## S021: two-note musical intervals, exact ratios and 12-TET

- Under **Хөг / Tuning**, choose the first sub-experiment **Интервал / Interval** (moved here in S022); the original close-frequency beat experiment is unchanged under Combine Waves. Presets **1:1, 2:1, 3:2, 4:3, 5:4, 6:5** are unison, octave, perfect fifth, perfect fourth, major third and minor third. The lower fundamental is adjustable from **110 to 440 Гц**, initially 220 Гц; the upper frequency follows the selected preset and tuning. Selecting any control is silent until Play.
- **Энгийн харьцаа / Just ratio** uses exactly `f₂/f₁ = p/q`. The first common harmonic is the lower note's `p`th partial and upper note's `q`th partial, `p f₁ = q f₂`. The shared period `T* = q/f₁ = p/f₂` contains `q` complete lower cycles and `p` upper cycles. **12-TET** uses `2^(s/12)` with the nearest standard semitone span and displays cents discrepancy and `|p f₁ − q f₂|`, the frequency difference of the once-coincident partials. Unison and octave remain exact in either tuning; other presets generally do not have an exact joint period under 12-TET.
- **Цэвэр / Pure** gives two pure sine fundamentals. **Гармониктай / Rich** synthesizes eight harmonic sine components per note at amplitudes proportional to `1/n`, conservatively normalized before Web Audio `PeriodicWave` playback. Thus the upper shared partials are **not present** in Pure mode, while Rich can expose their coincidence or slow/fast near-overlap beating. Two oscillators feed the same local recording bus; each is limited to 0.12 gain at maximum volume (combined steady-state bound 0.24), with smooth pitch changes and a crossfade when changing source color.
- **Wave plot:** three traces show the separate lower and upper waves and their normalized sum over the just-ratio shared-period reference window. The caption carefully distinguishes an exact repeat from a **nonperiodic 12-TET comparison**; it never labels the reference window an actual 12-TET period. **Spectrum:** two harmonic ladders, colored blue and orange, with markers on the first shared/near-shared pair. Small 12-TET discrepancies may be below the plotted frequency resolution; the exact numeric difference is shown as text instead. In Pure mode the graph contains only fundamentals, with no invented upper partials. Plots are predictions of the same synthesis recipe, not speaker measurements.
- A challenge asks students to predict the common period and first shared harmonics for 4:3. The explanation distinguishes **overtones within one note** from **fundamental ratios of two notes** and does not claim that integer ratios alone determine perceived pleasantness. [sound-intervals.js](sound-intervals.js) is an independent pure math/audio-coefficient module, with [numeric tests](sound-intervals.test.cjs) and [UI/recorder tests](sound-ui.test.cjs). No new external dependencies, microphone or autoplay.

## S022: Tuning / Хөг — interval comparison and fifth-chain scale building

- The sixth top-level category **Хөг / Tuning** began with **Интервал / Interval** (the existing S021 comparison, preserved) and **Шатлал бүтээх / Build a scale** (now **3-хязгаар / 3-limit**). They originally had a large Play button immediately below the sub-tabs. Later revisions added **Тааруулах / Tune** and **5-хязгаар / 5-limit** and, by S031, four sub-tabs each with two play/stop choices. The former Combine Waves choices are unchanged except that Interval moved out.
- **Generate notes from an exact fifth chain:** choose `k=-down,…,up` and pitch classes given by `(3/2)^k` brought into `[1,2)` by powers of two. Controls extend or shorten either end independently, with at most twelve notes. Built-in chains include five pitches made **upward**, **centered around the reference**, or **mostly downward**, plus seven- and twelve-note examples. Each preset is a contiguous section of the same fifth lattice; which frequency is considered the reference is separate from which pitch is chosen as the tonic.
- **Change the tonic without changing the collection:** select one of the generated rational pitch classes; the same notes are reordered upward from the selected tonic, and the graph highlights that note. The visible ordered adjacent-interval sequence rotates accordingly. For example, if the five pitch classes are labeled `C–D–E–G–A`, the C tonic yields a major pentatonic scale, while A yields the relative minor pentatonic; choosing G is another mode of that same pitch collection. Likewise a seven-pitch collection supports seven modal choices without retuning any of its pitch classes. Note letters are an optional interpretation, **not** a required input to generate the collection.
- **Listen and inspect:** the original large button played an ascending octave-constrained melody, reached the tonic one octave higher, and descended back to the chosen tonic; S031 also offers a simultaneous chord on the adjacent button. Playback uses locally generated, gently enveloped tones with a quiet second harmonic; Web Audio buffers share the REC/WAV bus. Pitch classes are displayed on a **logarithmic octave circle**, with exact fractions as labels (step numbers for a crowded twelve-note circle), and a separate **fifth-chain diagram** tracing the generation order. Numerically show the selected tonic's Hz, the successive semitone-like gaps in cents and the signed difference between `N` fifths and their nearest integer number of octaves. For `N=12`, this is the **Pythagorean comma**, about +23.46 cents. For five or seven fifths, the analogous difference is shown mathematically but is not a claimed closure of the tuning cycle.
- Model and audio generation reside in [sound-scales.js](sound-scales.js), with exact rational tests in [sound-scales.test.cjs](sound-scales.test.cjs) and browser-interaction tests in [sound-ui.test.cjs](sound-ui.test.cjs). There are no external libraries, uploads, autoplay, sustained sound on navigation, or newly introduced live microphone features. These experiments explore exact **Pythagorean** fifth chains; other just ratios such as `5:4`, mixed limit-5 lattices, adaptive retuning and score composition remain future work.

## S023: Octave spiral and Fourier cartoon

- The octave **spiral** replaces the pitch-class circle. Its angle is `2π log₂(f/f_reference)` and each new exact `3:2` fifth moves **210.5865° clockwise** (or −149.4135° modulo a turn). Radial distance tracks unwrapped logarithmic frequency, so positive fifth steps spiral outward and negative ones inward. One complete turn is an octave; the same angular ray represents octave-equivalent pitches. Labels `k = 0, ±1, …` expose the order of generating fifths, which differs from the pitches sorted inside one octave. The reference `k=0` is amber, the selectable tonic green, and the actively played note gains a gold halo. A square responsive canvas makes several turns readable on narrow screens.
- The former redundant horizontal chain is replaced by a **schematic harmonic row chart**: each successive `k` occupies a higher row; the x-axis is logarithmic frequency **before octave reduction**, measured relative to the lowest generated fifth. A filled circle marks a fundamental; diamonds mark the *possible harmonic frequencies* `2f, …, 8f` of a harmonically rich note. Vertical green connectors align `3f_lower = 2f_upper` for each adjacent `3:2` pair. Pale gray circles **left of the fundamental** are virtual octave-folded reference copies `f/2`, `f/4`, …, *not* real subharmonics or audio components. In particular, the graph is a conceptual Fourier cartoon, **not a measurement or the actual spectrum** of the current scale playback (which synthesizes only the fundamental and a quiet second partial).
- During ascending and descending melody playback, the displayed note label and both graphics follow the **Web Audio context clock** and highlight the actual sounding pitch. The upper tonic octave is explicitly identified, and the highlight clears when a note's short envelope ends, playback stops, a tab changes, or the sound naturally finishes. Separate model and mock-browser tests cover the 210.6° angle, shared partials, note-order/pitch-order distinction, timing, and cleanup. No additional tuning ratios were introduced; mixed `5:4` / `3:2` experiments remain later work.

## S024: Octave pitch sweep and octave-copy interpretation

- The spiral now includes subtle radial spokes through every generated note. Walking along the spiral follows signed fifth steps, while moving **clockwise around the center** from the chosen tonic follows ascending **pitch classes** within one octave. The active pitch's spoke turns gold. These are complementary orders; the faint spokes make pitch order legible even across several spiral turns.
- **5 · Down / 5 · Доош** is now literally four descending fifths from `k=0`, giving `k = −4,…,0`. Previously it meant three downward and one upward step, which contradicted the button label. The displayed octave-reduced pitch ratios can still be numerically above `1/1`: being a downward fifth *before* octave folding is different from having a lower frequency *within* the chosen octave.
- The Fourier cartoon highlights a **tonic-anchored one-octave window** in pale green, from `F_tonic` through `2F_tonic`. Every generated note has one marker in this window, either its original fundamental or an octave-transposed gray copy (possibly to the right of the original for downward fifths). Read these window markers **left to right** to recover the scale's audible ascending order; choosing another tonic shifts the window and changes the ordering. Gray dots are *playable pitch equivalents under octave transposition*, not extra simultaneous overtones or invented spectral components. The diamonds remain illustrative possible harmonics, not a measured/audio spectrum.
- On Play, **only the current note marker in the green octave window** receives a gold highlight; the horizontal row and original fundamental stay unchanged. The upper tonic is the corresponding marker at the right boundary (`2F_tonic`), so highlighting proceeds from left to right then reverses as the melody descends. Timing still comes from the Web Audio context, and Stop/navigation/natural end clear all highlights. Tests check this behavior and exact tonic-window pitches. Mixed `5:4` tuning was added subsequently in S026.

## S025: Optional fifth-chain return-gap demonstration

- **Буцах зөрүүг харуулах / Show the return gap** is an off-by-default checkbox in **Хөг → 3-хязгаар** (formerly Шатлал бүтээх). It adds a *ghost* fifth-chain step just outside the generated collection: `+5` after five upward notes, `−5` after five downward notes, or `+7` after the twelve-note `−5…+6` chain. A **gold dashed spiral continuation** and an empty ghost marker distinguish the extra theoretical pitch from the actual scale. On a common outer octave ring a short gold arc compares its angle to the starting pitch; a small **magnified cents ruler** below the Fourier cartoon makes even a few cents legible. The old numeric return-discrepancy stat is hidden with the overlay until checked; all additions disappear when unchecked.
- The shown gap is the difference between the starting pitch and the ghost after octave adjustment. For **five fifths** the near return is **±90.22 cents**, approximately **27.07°** of one octave, with opposite signs for all-up and all-down. For **twelve fifths**, the error is the **Pythagorean comma**, `3^12 / 2^19 = 531441/524288`, or **+23.46 cents = +7.04°**. The former is not called the Pythagorean comma; other chain lengths can be inspected without claiming that they nearly close.
- The separate **Hear both pitches / Хоёр нотыг сонсох** button plays the starting pitch and octave-adjusted ghost as quiet simultaneous sinusoids for about **3.2 seconds**. Students can hear beats caused by the frequency difference; this is a comparison of two notes, not an additional scale tone or measured spectrum. It joins the shared audio recorder, never autoplays, and stops on the global Stop, unchecked toggle, parameter changes, tab switch, or page hide. It does not move the main large scale Play button.
- The **Fourier cartoon frequency window** is framed tightly around all original fundamentals with roughly the next two nearby harmonics, rather than displaying a long, compressed tail of high harmonics. Distant diamonds remain mathematically implied but fall outside the view; the green one-octave window and single highlighted sounding marker remain unchanged.

## S026: 5-limit ratio lattice and syntonic comma

- **Хөг / Tuning** originally offered **Интервал / Interval**, **3-хязгаар / 3-limit**, and **5-хязгаар / 5-limit**; S027–S029 inserted **Тааруулах / Tune** between the first two. The large Play button originally used here was replaced by two compact playback controls in S030.
- The 5-limit model uses coordinates `(m,n)` for `(3/2)^m (5/4)^n`, always folded into `[1,2)`. All shown fractions use exact integer numerators/denominators. The triangular grid's horizontal edges are fifths (`3:2`), northeast edges are pure major thirds (`5:4`), and the remaining edge is a minor third (`6:5`). A dot can be added or removed by pointer or by focusing the canvas, navigating with arrow keys, and selecting with Enter/Space. A maximum of twelve notes avoids overcrowding.
- Presets: pure **C–E–G** major, **E–G–B** minor, **G–B–D** major (with D voiced above G for the chord), **D–F–A** minor, a seven-note just-intonation collection, and a fifth-only subset. Melodic playback walks selected notes in ascending *octave-folded* pitch order; chord playback sounds the selected notes together with octave shifts relative to each preset's first chosen note. Changing presets and parameters is silent. A default C reference of 261.63 Hz aligns the initial C major note names with concert tuning; changing C transposes the whole lattice.
- **Syntonic comma 81:80 (~21.51 cents)** was originally a separate experiment comparing E at `5/4` versus `81/64` (four fifths), or D at `10/9` versus `9/8`. S030 moves these paths into the normal preset picker and reserves path and cents graphics for genuine near-return pairs selected by the student. Optional same-12-TET guides remain off on reset.
- Playback uses **one-shot generated audio buffers** through the same recorder bus, bounded gain, audio clock tracking and cleanup on Stop, changing tuning/mode, hiding the page, or natural completion. The charts are explanatory mathematics rather than recorded-sound FFTs. Bilingual text, independent model tests and browser-simulation tests cover this tuning.

## S028: Single-ratio listening exercises and lightweight sound choices

- The ear tab now offers **seven independent ratio tasks** from C4 = 261.63 Hz: `3:2`, `5:4`, `4:3`, `2:1`, `3:4`, `2:3`, and `1:2`. Multi-step chains, intermediate-fold controls and history are removed. A different reproducible offset (roughly 16–23 cents) hides the true target behind each centered ±60-cent slider. The **adjustment slider sits immediately below the task picker** and above the sound and listening controls; no task or parameter change autoplays.
- Choose one of **pure sine**, **rich harmonics**, **simplified guitar pluck**, or **simplified piano strike**, then **in sequence** (default) or **together**. The realtime sound engine uses at most eight independent partial oscillators per voice, bounded gains, small envelopes and no precomputed audio buffers. Inharmonicity is added to the piano partial ratios as an illustrative model; these timbres are not physical instrument samples. Audio always routes through the existing recorder bus and stops on tab switch, explicit Stop, or page hide.
- The guided harmonic plot shows ordinary unmarked bars for both notes, but **no labeled moving 3f/2f dots**, no highlighted matching pair and no magnified answer diagram. Listen-only hides the bars entirely. The adjacent C3–C5 pitch plot deliberately hides the tuned target until **Шалгах / Check**; then it shows the resulting pitch and, for notes outside C4–C5, the octave copy obtained by `×2` or `÷2`. Downward exercises thereby introduce the same modulo-octave operation used in the 3-limit and 5-limit tabs.
- A Check gives the exact ideal frequency, the player's frequency, signed cents error and predicted beating of the relevant partials **if present**. The grade is a friendly ±5/±15-cent comparison rather than an assessment of musical hearing. Canvas redraws are coalesced to one animation frame for slider input, and resetting unmodified backing-store sizes was removed to reduce the previously reported UI stalls. ## S033: Historical keyboard temperaments — five systems on the circle of fifths

- **Хөг → Темперамент / Tuning → Temperaments** is the fifth Tuning experiment. The selector compares **12-tone equal temperament (12-TET), Pythagorean tuning, quarter-comma meantone, Werckmeister III, and Vallotti**. A twelve-node circle colors individual fifths: green for pure 3:2, blue for narrowed, amber for a wolf fifth. Tap a note or use Left/Right arrows on the focused canvas to choose a chord root. The second diagram plots each fifth's **signed deviation from pure 3:2 in cents**, with an expandable numerical table. For every model the 12 circle edges add to exactly **8,400 cents (seven octaves)**.
- The fifth circle follows **C–G–D–A–E–B–F♯–C♯–G♯–E♭–B♭–F–C**. The Pythagorean system has eleven pure fifths and one compressed wolf at G♯–E♭; quarter-comma meantone narrows eleven fifths by one quarter **syntonic comma** (81:80), leaving an enlarged wolf at that same seam. The difference between twelve pure fifths and seven octaves is the **Pythagorean comma** (approximately 23.46 cents). These two commas have different sizes; they must not be confused.
- **Werckmeister III** has C–G, G–D, D–A, B–F♯ narrowed by **¼ Pythagorean comma** each; all eight other fifths are pure. **Vallotti (common modern reconstruction)** has F–C, C–G, G–D, D–A, A–E, E–B narrowed by **⅙ Pythagorean comma** each; the remaining six fifths are pure. These explicit allocations—not labels inferred from subjective sound descriptions—produce all pitch frequencies. Source documentation: [Scholtz (1998), Music Theory Online](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.98.4.4/mto.98.4.4.scholtz_essay.html) for meantone's chain/wolf, [Yamamoto (2015), York thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/12055/21/PhD%20Masumi%20Yamamoto.pdf) for Werckmeister III and Vallotti and [Dixon et al. (2012)](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2012/JASMAN1311878_1.pdf) for the fifth distribution overview.
- Select any of twelve chord roots and **major or minor**, then tap **Ээлжлэн / In sequence** or **Хамт / Together**. Tap the same active button to stop; changing playback mode replaces it. Chord cards show actual Hz, names, signed cents deviations from matching 12-TET notes, and the chord's two intervals. All systems are normalized to **A4 = 440 Hz** and use the existing modest harmonic-rich, sampled 5-limit voice and shared WAV/Stop bus. They illustrate *tuning* differences, **not** historical keyboard timbres, octave stretch, adaptive harmony, split enharmonic keys or acoustically calibrated historical performances. The C♯/D♭, G♯/A♭ etc. distinction is folded into **twelve** fixed classes, and historical authors/performers may use different variants of a named temperament.
- Tests: [sound-temperaments.test.cjs](sound-temperaments.test.cjs) checks closure, exact fifth allocation, both comma definitions, wolf placement, A4 normalization, octave transposition and chord ratios. [sound-ui.test.cjs](sound-ui.test.cjs) checks fifth tab visibility, bilingual text, interactive plots/root/key/quality, strict no-autoplay, exclusive playback, shared audio routing, volume, recording-bus behavior, pending audio cancellation and stop on navigation.

## S020A: flute blowing strength — first source/filter model

- **Лимбэ / Flute** now has a **Үлээлтийн хүч / Blowing strength** slider (0–100%), visible only for flute. At zero the synthetic air jet and breath noise are silent. As the slider increases, the held tone gets stronger and progressively richer in upper harmonics, with more soft breath noise. The change is smoothly applied to the current note without starting a new oscillator; pitch, note snap, attack/release, master Stop and WAV capture keep their existing behavior. Violin and morin khuur remain unchanged in this first S020 slice.
- The compact *illustrative* audio path is **sinusoidal bore-pressure proxy → smoothly controlled input → asymmetric polynomial jet nonlinearity → high-pass (70 Hz) → low-pass (3,400 Hz) → note envelope**. The high-pass removes the non-radiating DC component introduced by the asymmetric jet; the low-pass approximates a frequency-dependent bore/radiation color, **not** full tube resonances. Filtered synthetic breath noise is added separately. Both filters use Web Audio biquads at (Q=1/\sqrt{2}\).
- [sound-sustain.js](sound-sustain.js) contains the jet polynomial, blowing control map and the *predicted* harmonic amplitudes **and phases after both filters**. The 20 ms waveform and eight harmonic bars use those same coefficients; Web Audio produces the corresponding nonlinear filtered waveform, subject to device sample rate, WaveShaper interpolation/oversampling and live parameter smoothing. The random breath component is deliberately absent from the periodic plots. The 1.2-second attack/release chart remains a **normalized envelope**, not a measurement of absolute loudness.
- This is **not** a closed-loop flute or measured-instrument simulation. In an acoustic flute, the jet and the resonant air column feed back into each other and may change registers when overblown. Here the bore frequency is imposed by an oscillator so pitch stays stable while the jet color changes. The broader jet-delay/feedback, noise and pressure controls in the [STK flute description](https://chuck.cs.princeton.edu/doc/reference/ugens-stk.html#Flute) are a conceptual reference, not code reused here. A fuller physical model and listening comparisons remain future work.

**S020A.1 listening adjustment:** The original generated breath-turbulence channel sounded too prominent compared with the pitched flute. Its gain is now eight times lower (roughly 18 dB in amplitude terms) at every nonzero blowing strength, while its dependence on breath strength and the periodic jet/filter tone stay unchanged. A regression test bounds the peak synthetic noise input relative to the modeled fundamental at 110–880 Hz, even before noise low-pass filtering. This is a listening-oriented correction, not a calibrated recording of a real flute; the owner will evaluate the live sound on speakers and headphones.

## S020B: bowed-string stick–slip and body response — first source/filter model

- **Хийл / Violin** and **Морин хуур / Morin khuur** now reveal a separate **Нумын даралт / Bow pressure** slider (0–100%); the flute's blowing slider remains visible only for flute. At zero, both periodic tone and separate random bow texture are silent. Stronger bowing increases amplitude and makes the upper harmonics sharper, without restarting the held note. Pitch, note snap, attack/release, touch/keyboard hold/toggle, global Stop, WAV capture and bilingual labels continue to work.
- **Idealized contact motion:** The bow-point string velocity alternates between a long *stick* interval and a shorter backward *slip* interval (a Helmholtz-like periodic shape). For a slip fraction $d$, we use a zero-mean rectangular velocity proxy $v(\theta)\propto d-\mathbf{1}_{0\leq\theta<2\pi d}$. Its $n$th Fourier amplitude is proportional to $2|\sin(n\pi d)|/(n\pi)$. Two Gaussian-smoothed versions give a gentler and a sharper slip transition. The pressure slider blends those versions with smoothly changing gains, so a playing note changes color without wavetable replacement clicks. These are **prescribed waveforms**, not a numerical friction force law or a self-oscillating bow/string model.
- **Illustrative body response:** Each waveform passes through two fixed Web Audio `peaking` biquads and a low-pass biquad. The violin's source has a shorter slip fraction and higher body/radiation emphasis; the morin-khuur-inspired model has a longer slip fraction and lower-frequency body color. The filter centers and gains were selected for distinct educational contrasts, **not derived from acoustic measurements** of either instrument. A very quiet, pressure-dependent filtered stochastic layer represents residual bow contact noise. Its gain is much lower than the earlier S016 noise level; the random layer is omitted from the ideal plots.
- **Audio/plot agreement:** [sound-sustain.js](sound-sustain.js) predicts all 24 complex harmonic coefficients *after* the three body filters. The plotted 20 ms waveform and 24 bars use those coefficients. Playback combines two phase-aligned `PeriodicWave` oscillators with Web Audio normalization explicitly disabled, and uses the matching peaking/low-pass filters. Tests independently render the stick–slip sources through discrete-time biquads and recover harmonic phases and amplitudes. Differences remain possible through hardware sample rate, oscillator anti-aliasing, live parameter smoothing, and acoustic playback.
- **Scope and references:** This is a computationally cheap *source/filter approximation* to one aspect of friction-excited bowed strings. A full digital waveguide would couple moving string waves to a nonlinear bow-contact force, and measured bridge/body impulse responses would be needed for convincing instrumental identity. Compare the idealized physical explanation in [Euphonics §9.2.1](https://euphonics.org/9-2-1-time-domain-simulation-of-a-bowed-string/) and the [Csound `wgbow` reference](https://csound.com/manual/opcodes/wgbow/). The morin-khuur source and resonator settings are explicitly provisional; see also [Zhu et al. (2025)](https://tns.ewapub.com/article/view/29758) for an independent morin-khuur modeling attempt. No code, samples or measured coefficients were copied from these references.

## S020C: vocal resonances and focused overtones for the theremin

- The first two theremin colors remain **Цэвэр / Pure** and **Баян / Rich**. The old fixed eight-partial “voice-like” and “overtones” presets have become **Эгшиг авиа / Vowels** and **Дээд гармоник / Focus overtone**, each with a small, conditional control row. Both use **one** glottal-like periodic source with 24 descending harmonic amplitudes, not two independent voices. The pitch and volume pad still behave as before; the Siren is unchanged.
- **Vowels:** choose А, Э, И, О or У. Three peaking biquad filters emphasize illustrative vocal-tract resonances (formants) at *fixed frequencies in Hz*, followed by a lowpass that softens the source. Unlike the old fixed-number harmonic recipe, moving the fundamental shifts the harmonic lines while the vowel filter centers stay put. The formants move smoothly if the vowel changes during playback without starting another oscillator. The approximate vowel centers/Q/gains are *pedagogical choices*, not Mongolian speech measurements. Expect more intelligible vowel coloration for a low note, about 120–350 Hz, than near the pad's maximum of 1,600 Hz; these are sustained vowel-like sounds without consonants, pitch microvariation, vocal-fold physiology or aspiration.
- **Focused overtone:** select an integer n=2,…,12. A narrow peaking filter follows the already present harmonic n×f₀ while keeping the fundamental drone; its gain is limited by a simple n-dependent rule. The same **one oscillator** supplies all harmonics, the selected focus updates in-place with n or pitch, and the spectrum highlights the selected bar. This illustrates the source/filter mechanism underlying *some* khöömei styles (especially Sygyt); it is not a full model of a particular throat singer, mouth geometry, or other forms of multiphonic phonation. At high pitches the focused harmonic can exceed the device's useful bandwidth, so try a low base pitch.
- **Audio/plots:** the Web Audio source is a normalized `PeriodicWave`, passed through three vowel peaking filters + lowpass or one focused peaking filter + lowpass, with quieter gain headroom for boosted formants. [sound-motion.js](sound-motion.js) computes the same discrete-time biquad transfer functions, including harmonic phase, so the 20 ms waveform and the first 16 harmonic bars predict the stationary filtered tone at the browser's sample rate (except native band-limiting and smoothing transients). Pure/rich colors still use their original periodic harmonics; changing categories, stopping, recording and bilingual labels retain their existing behavior.
- **Why one voice can sound like two:** a low fundamental f₀ and an independently emphasized upper harmonic n×f₀ can be perceived as distinct pitches. The high line is already inside the single periodic glottal source, not a separately sung note. [Bergevin et al., *eLife* (2020)](https://elifesciences.org/articles/50476) combined recordings, dynamic MRI and vocal-tract modeling of Tuvan Sygyt overtone singing; [KTH case study (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34860148/) reports complementary source/filter analysis. Neither paper's data or software were copied into this educational prototype.

## Audio headroom and device limits

- Bounded gains across the five top-level modes: Pure Tones peak 0.30, modeled strikes 0.21 per voice (maximum four simultaneous), held notes 0.22 before mild texture (**flute's nonlinear jet and bowed source/body peaks can briefly be roughly 1.1 times this level** at maximum input), Moving Pitch **0.24 before filters** (0.12 for vowel excitation, 0.1152 for overtone excitation), beat pairs 0.12 per oscillator (0.24 summed), harmonic builder 0.24 and noise explorer 0.20. Vocal filters can increase the instantaneous peak **beyond their input gain**, so the 0.24 number is not a hard ceiling on filtered sound. The master output isn't a dynamic compressor; headphone/device gain still matters. Existing volume slider defaults remain unchanged; model waveforms and spectra still show mathematical signals rather than a calibrated loudness measurement.
- These are bounded **linear audio amplitudes**, not calibrated loudness or device output in decibels. Small phone speakers often cannot reproduce low bass even when the generated signal is louder, and raising gain does not restore frequencies they cannot physically produce. Start quietly when switching to headphones or larger speakers; there is no automatic loudness normalization or bass enhancement.

- All modeled voices route to one shared gain bus that goes to the speakers and, while recording, to an [AudioWorklet capture processor](sound-recorder-worklet.js). The capture processor sends bounded Float32 sample chunks to the [recorder and WAV encoder](sound-recorder.js) and emits **silence** on its connected output so playback is not doubled. Worklet timing, the 15-second cap, PCM headers and playback/control lifecycle have automated tests in [sound-recorder.test.cjs](sound-recorder.test.cjs) and [sound-ui.test.cjs](sound-ui.test.cjs).
- This records **generated app audio**, not the microphone, external music, or ambient sounds. A browser without AudioWorklet support can still use the sound modes; the header recorder reports an error when recording is unavailable. On narrow phones, the control becomes an icon-only button with an accessible label.

## Future extension boundaries

Keep three concepts separate:

1. Pitch identity: symbolic note or explicitly entered Hz.
2. Tuning context: P1 resolves symbolic notes through 12-TET only; later just/Pythagorean/alternative scales change how notes resolve into frequencies, not the written score.
3. Sound recipe: waveform/timbre, modeled drums, piano and plucked strings, **held flute and bowed strings**, eight-partial harmonic mixtures, three noise colors, moving pitch, close-frequency interference, **two-note intervals**, and **fifth-chain scale melodies** now; later richer articulation, modulation and score-driven instruments.

**Next candidates, not yet shipped:** After S022's Pythagorean fifth-chain construction: mixed `3:2` / `5:4` ratio lattices, adaptive just intonation in chords and Compose integration. Following S020B listening, a true nonlinear bow/string feedback waveguide, bow-position control, vibrato and **measured instrument body** responses could improve violin and morin khuur substantially. A stylized **cymbal** (rapidly decaying plate modes and filtered noise), **steel pan** (tuned plate modes) and snare/other modeled drums belong with Strike/Pluck. Piano still needs a physically modeled nonlinear hammer collision, measured soundboard coupling and a damper controlled by note-off. **S020C adds simple vowel filters and overtone focus**, but true singing synthesis, dynamic vocal-fold/tract coupling and measured formants remain future work. Held sounds already supply note-on and note-off with adjustable envelopes; future Compose should schedule those same recipes by note duration, not duplicate the synthesis engine. Acoustic fidelity and touch behavior still need direct device listening tests.

Later work will add Compose and Analyze **when usable**, rather than displaying disabled tabs now. Score-wide tunings belong in Compose; Explore tunings stay in Settings and only affect note snapping. A score can reuse piano, plucked string, percussion, harmonic mixtures and later held sounds with independent note-on/note-off controls; it will not need its own piano keyboard graphics.

## Scientific/technical limits

**S033 additional live check:** Enter Хөг → Темперамент. Compare Werckmeister III (four narrowed fifths), Vallotti (six), 12-TET (twelve), Pythagorean (compressed G♯–E♭ wolf) and quarter-comma meantone (enlarged G♯–E♭ wolf). C major has a pure meantone third; G♯ major does not. Confirm the circle, table and deviation bars agree, and the A4 reference is 440 Hz throughout. Tap a circle note/arrow keys, compare major/minor and root transpositions, play in sequence/together, stop/switch, record WAV, test EN/MN and a 390px mobile viewport. Changing systems or notes must not autoplay or leave an old buffer sounding.

- Harmonic plots show mathematical model magnitudes (sometimes normalized, sometimes absolute); actual speaker/headphone responses, Nyquist filtering and hearing vary. The waveforms model harmonic phase, while magnitude-only bars omit it.
- There is no maximum listening-time or dB calibration; users should start at low volume, particularly with headphones.
- Higher frequencies may be outside the effective bandwidth of a device; avoid presenting them as proof of a child's individual hearing range.
- The local FFT analyzes a mathematically generated signal, not live microphone or sound-card audio. A short-time spectrum necessarily trades time resolution for frequency resolution; this release fixes its window at 128 ms, especially relevant for very low drum frequencies.
- This release has WAV export of locally generated sounds, three preset guitar chord strums, two-note tuning comparison, and fifth-chain scale playback; it has no microphone analysis, recording of external sounds, editable score or unrestricted melody composition.
- Guitar uses a compact delayed-feedback ideal string with an illustrative pick transient; piano uses modal, inharmonic, velocity-dependent damped partials and near-detuned unisons with a **broad static soundboard coloration**, not full measured/coupled resonances. Both omit much of a real instrument's body response. Piano **Товших** only approximates a damper returning at a fixed short interval; the model has no continuously controlled key-release event, sustain pedal, nonlinear hammer–string collision or sympathetic resonance. The drum uses a limited sum of circular-membrane modes; it does not solve membrane dynamics with shell, air-cavity coupling or nonlinear head tension, and the current strike point is fixed.
- Flute now uses a nonlinear jet-like waveshaper, periodic driving oscillator and two simple filters with breath-dependent strength. The violin and morin-khuur-inspired models use **prescribed** smoothed stick–slip source velocities, 24 periodic harmonics and separate fixed body filter chains; they do **not** compute a dynamically coupled friction law or measured bridge/body resonances. None includes complete excitation/resonator feedback or instrument-specific articulation. The plots predict the **filtered periodic tone** but omit random breath/bow texture, Web Audio oversampling and device response. Attack and release remain modeled gain curves. The envelope graph assumes an *illustrative* fixed 1.2-second hold, while audible notes sustain until release or Stop; acoustic device testing is still required.
- Theremin timbres are **modeled moving-frequency harmonic oscillators**, not actual theremin, human-voice or throat-singing recordings. S020C provides illustrative **fixed-frequency vowel formants** and a narrow, pitch-tracking **overtone focus**, but no anatomical vocal-tract waveguide, independently controlled tract motion, unvoiced consonants, vocal-fold dynamics or calibrated phonetics. The siren uses a pure sine; its graphical path is a mathematical prediction. The theremin's bars show an **instantaneous ideal filtered periodic spectrum**; rapid pitch motion adds transient spectral spread, and real devices change the perceived color.
- The beat plot is the mathematical sum of equal-amplitude, steady sine waves. Retuning live in Web Audio briefly glides frequency; transient audio, room acoustics, and the listening device may add artifacts that the ideal two-line spectrum cannot show. At the upper end of the Δf slider, a child may hear roughness rather than distinct individual pulses.
- The harmonic builder uses eight sine partials with common phase and Web Audio's periodic-wave normalization. An instrument's timbre also depends on phase, transient envelope, noise and inharmonicity, which these eight sliders do not model. At high fundamentals some upper partials may be above the effective bandwidth of the speaker.
- Pink/brown noise colors are illustrative stochastic models, not standardized noise sources or calibrated spectral-density measurements. The generated buffer is finite and looped; the plotted response is an analytical power-law approximation (including idealized low-pass attenuation), not a measured FFT of the actual generated or filtered buffer. The displayed noise waveform uses a two-pole smoothing approximation for explanatory purposes; the browser playback uses a Web Audio biquad. Colors may sound very different on phone speakers or low-frequency-limited devices.
