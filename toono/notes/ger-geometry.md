# Ger proportions

## What the tool uses now

The presets in `engine.js` are rough estimates made when the tool was
written. They were not taken from a source.

| Preset | Floor radius R | Wall h_w | Toono height H | Toono diameter | Uni |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 khana | 2.3 m | 1.45 m | 2.3 m | 1.2 m | 60 |
| 5 khana | 2.7 m | 1.5 m | 2.45 m | 1.3 m | 81 |
| 6 khana | 3.1 m | 1.55 m | 2.6 m | 1.4 m | 96 |

Only R, h_w, H and the toono diameter affect the light. The uni count is
used only to draw evenly spaced uni lines. The door is drawn as a gap
about 0.84 m wide and does not change the geometry.

## A traditional rule set

A 2010 post on the Ари-Мгл blog (see sources) describes the ger with the
toono as the unit of measure. In its terms, with D the toono diameter:

- the door is as wide as the toono: door width = D,
- the column (багана) is 1.5 times the door height,
- the floor diameter is 4D,
- the uni length is three toono radii, that is 1.5D,
- each khana has 14–16 heads (толгой). The "small four" (бага дөрвөн хана)
  has four khana with 14 heads, the "large six" six khana with 16 heads,
- the smallest ger has three khana and the five-khana ger is the most
  common,
- the ger that matches the sun clock exactly is the small four with 60 uni,
- each uni hole is named after one of the twelve animals.

Let h be the door height. The wall height is also h: the door frame stands
in the wall line and the uni must rest on a level ring, so the wall head and
the top of the door are at the same height.

## Consistency of the rules

The floor (4D) and the column (1.5h) already fix the shape. The wall radius
is 2D. The roof rises 0.5h from the wall head to the toono, over a
horizontal run of 2D − D/2 = 1.5D. The uni length is then determined, so
the rule "uni = 1.5D" is a check rather than a free choice.

**Taken literally the rules give a flat roof.** A uni of 1.5D spanning a
horizontal run of 1.5D has no room to rise. The part of the uni inserted
into the toono and its end beyond the wall head make the needed length
longer, not shorter.

**A toono ring of realistic width resolves it.** If D is the inner
opening, and the uni enter holes in the outer face of a ring of radial
width w, the run is 1.5D − w. With h = 1.5 m, the ring width needed for
uni = 1.5D is:

| D | Floor 4D | Ring width needed | Roof slope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.9 m | 3.6 m | 23 cm | 34° |
| 1.2 m | 4.8 m | 16 cm | 25° |
| 1.4 m | 5.6 m | 14 cm | 21° |

So the rules agree for a toono of about 1.2–1.4 m with an ordinary ring.
They do not agree for a toono as narrow as a usual door (about 0.9 m), and
such a ger would be far too small. "Door width = toono diameter" is the
weakest rule. Real doors are narrower than the toono.

## Comparison with a seller's specifications

A Mongolian ger seller (mongoliabusiness.mn, see sources) lists for its
standard 4-khana ger: wall 150 cm, door 150 × 90 cm, toono 140 cm, 64 uni,
column 195 cm, centre height 210 cm, diameter 490 cm. Its 5-khana and
6-khana pages list toono 150 cm and 180 cm, 81 and 96 uni, and columns of
236 cm and 294 cm.

- The door height equals the wall height, as argued above.
- The door (0.9 m) is much narrower than the toono (1.4 m).
- The floor is 3.5D rather than 4D.
- The column is 1.3h, lower than the rule's 1.5h.
- The tables are not fully consistent. The 5-khana page gives a column of
  236 cm but a centre height of 230 cm, and the 6-khana diameter is given
  as 6.50 m on one page and 7.30 m on another. The numbers are rough.

## Reach: how high is the toono?

The author's recollection: in a 5-khana ger, a tall person standing on a
low chair barely reaches the top of the toono. Even in a small ger an
average person rarely reaches the rim of the toono, let alone its middle,
from the floor. The toono has a shallow dome about 20 cm high above the
ring.

Standing reach is roughly 1.3 times body height: about 2.1 m at 160 cm,
2.2 m at 170 cm and 2.35 m at 180 cm (a rule of thumb, not a measurement).
The underside of the ring sits on the column.

- With the rule's column of 1.5h = 2.25 m, an average adult would just
  touch the rim. That is easier than remembered.
- With the seller's 1.95 m it would be easy. Their figure may not be
  measured to the toono.
- "Rarely reach the rim" needs a column of about 2.4 m or more, that is
  about 1.6h or higher.
- The 5-khana recollection fits a toono top near 3 m, close to the
  seller's taller columns for 5- and 6-khana gers.

A column cannot be a fixed multiple of the door height for every size: the
door stays about 1.5 m high while the toono and column grow with the ger.
The rule is best read as a rule for the small four.

## Keeping the uni rule with a taller column

A taller column makes the roof steeper. With the uni fixed at 1.5D, the
horizontal run must shorten, so the floor shrinks. With D = 1.3 m,
h = 1.5 m and a 12 cm ring:

| Column | Floor | Roof slope | Door angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5h = 2.25 m | 3.95D = 5.14 m | 23° | 29.3° |
| 1.6h = 2.40 m | 3.85D = 5.00 m | 28° | 30.1° |
| 1.65h = 2.47 m | 3.78D = 4.92 m | 30° | 30.7° |
| 1.7h = 2.55 m | 3.71D = 4.83 m | 33° | 31.3° |
| 1.8h = 2.70 m | 3.55D = 4.61 m | 38° | 32.7° |

The author's impressions, a floor a little under 4D and a column a little
over 1.5h, are exactly the trade the uni rule allows. A column of about
1.6–1.7h with a floor of about 3.7–3.85D keeps the uni at 1.5D with an
ordinary ring. It puts the rim just out of an average person's reach and
gives a floor of about 4.8–5.0 m, close to the seller's 4.9 m for a
4-khana ger. Beyond about 1.8h the roof becomes steeper than gers look.

## The uni count

- **60 = 12 × 5.** With 60 evenly spaced uni, each animal sector holds five
  and each uni marks 6°, which is 24 minutes in the fixed sectors. This fits
  the claim that the small four with 60 uni is the exact sun clock.
- **The door spans about one sector.** A door of width D in a floor of
  diameter kD subtends 2·arcsin(1/k) at the centre: 28.96° for k = 4 and
  30.5° for k = 3.8. So the door takes almost exactly one twelfth of the
  ring, and about five uni positions fall over it.
- **4 × 14 + 4 = 60.** Four khana with 14 heads give 56 uni, and four more
  on the door frame make 60.
- **Joins between khana.** With the door in the ring there are three joins
  between the four khana. If neighbouring end heads shared one uni, the
  khana would carry only 53 and the door would need 7, which do not fit in
  a 29–31° door. So the numbers suggest that each khana keeps all its heads
  at the joins, or that the counts are approximate. This is an inference
  from the numbers, not a description of practice.
- **The large six.** Six khana with 16 heads give 96 heads before the door,
  which is a multiple of 12 only without door uni. This fits the post
  naming only the small four as the exact sun clock.
- The seller lists 64 uni for its 4-khana ger, not 60.

## Proposed "small four" preset

Not yet implemented. Derived from the rules above with the author's
corrections:

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Toono inner diameter D | 1.3 m |
| Ring width w | 0.12 m |
| Door and wall height h | 1.5 m |
| Column | 1.65h ≈ 2.47 m |
| Floor diameter | ≈ 3.78D ≈ 4.92 m (from the uni rule) |
| Uni length | 1.5D = 1.95 m |
| Uni count | 60 (56 on the khana, 4 on the door frame) |

Suggested tool inputs: toono diameter, ring width, door height and column
height, with the floor following from the uni rule. Uni would be drawn at
an even 6° spacing, the ones over the door resting on the door frame, so
that "the light is on the n-th uni" could become a reading.

In the model, the light enters through the inner opening of the ring, so
the aperture radius is D/2 and its height is the column plus the ring
thickness. The model's roof cone runs to the inner rim, while real uni end
at the outer face of the ring. The difference is the ring width and moves
the uni–wall boundaries of the patch only slightly.
