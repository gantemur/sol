# Toono research notes

These notes record what was learned while building the ger sun clock
(«Гэрийн наран цаг», versions 0.1–0.5, November 2026). They are working
notes, not a publication. Each claim is marked as either a sourced
statement, a calculation that can be repeated, or a hypothesis. Hypotheses
are not established facts.

## Contents

## Main findings

1. No documented origin of the :40 boundaries was found. Under today's
   UTC+8 clock, a Horse hour of 11:40–13:40 is centred on 12:40, which is
   mean solar noon at 110°E. Mean noon is within 25 minutes of 12:40 in
   Ulaanbaatar and 14 of the 21 aimag centres, including the three western
   ones on UTC+7. It is 26–55 minutes late in the other seven, worst in
   Govi-Altai and Zavkhan, which are on UTC+8. The equation of time adds
   −14 to +16 minutes over the year. Before 1978, Ulaanbaatar was on UTC+7
   and mean noon there was 11:52, so the same boundaries would have centred
   Horse about 48 minutes after noon.
2. Where the clock is far from local solar time the convention fails.
   Lhasa keeps Beijing time at 91°E, so apparent noon falls near 13:40–14:10
   and the light reaches north only after Horse hour has ended.
3. The light patch on the floor is an exact copy of the toono disk, moved
   away from the Sun by H·cot(altitude), where H is the toono height. On the
   wall and the uni it is distorted. At low Sun it lands on the uni below
   the toono rim.
4. Equal-hour (sundial) lines lie in planes through the toono centre that
   contain the Earth's axis. On the floor they are straight lines that meet
   not at the centre but at a point near the door, H·cot(latitude) south of
   the centre.
5. A traditional rule set (door width = toono diameter D, floor 4D, column
   1.5 × door height, uni 1.5D) is over-determined. Taken literally it
   gives a flat roof. With a toono ring about 15 cm wide it becomes
   consistent. A slightly smaller floor and a slightly taller column, as
   the author remembers, keep it consistent.
6. With 60 uni, each uni marks 6° (24 minutes in fixed sectors), five per
   animal sector. A door of width D in a floor of about 4D spans about one
   sector (29–31°), which fits 4 × 14 khana heads plus 4 uni over the door.

## Open questions

- When did the :40 convention appear, and in which sources? If it predates
  1978, the longitude explanation would need UTC+7 and a reference near
  95°E instead.
- Does "Rabbit hour" begin at sunrise, at dawn, or is it simply the fixed
  5:40–7:40 slot? Which reading does traditional practice support?
- Do neighbouring khana share a head (and a uni) at their joins? How many
  uni rest on the door frame in practice?
- Real measurements of small four-khana gers: toono diameter, ring width,
  column height, floor diameter and uni length.
- Whether Govi-Altai and Zavkhan follow UTC+7 or UTC+8 in daily life. The
  time-zone database used by browsers puts them on UTC+8.
