# Double hours, the :40 convention and longitude

## The convention

The day is divided into twelve double hours named after the twelve
animals, with Mouse (Хулгана) at midnight and Horse (Морь) at noon. The
convention found today in dictionaries and calendars puts the boundaries
at :40 on the clock: Mouse 23:40–01:40, Ox 01:40–03:40, and so on, with
Horse 11:40–13:40. People follow it as a convention without an
explanation attached.

## Where :40 comes from: what was found

A search of Mongolian and English material found no documented origin for
the :40 boundaries. The following ideas were considered. All are
hypotheses.

1. **Longitude correction to the zone clock.** A Horse hour of 11:40–13:40
   is centred on 12:40. Mean solar noon is at 12:40 zone time 10° west of
   the zone's reference meridian: at 110°E under UTC+8, or at 95°E under
   UTC+7. Mongolia spans roughly 88–120°E, so a single 40-minute shift is
   a reasonable compromise for the country as a whole (see the table
   below).
2. **The clock in force when the convention appeared.** Ulaanbaatar was on
   UTC+7 from 1905 to the end of 1977 and on UTC+8 since 1978 (IANA time
   zone database). Under UTC+7, mean noon in Ulaanbaatar is 11:52 and the
   :40 boundaries would centre Horse 48 minutes after noon. So hypothesis 1
   fits the post-1978 clock. If the convention is older, it would point to
   a western reference near 95°E, or to some other origin.
3. **Sunrise.** The traditional day, as in the Mongol–Tibetan calendar,
   begins at sunrise. Some say Rabbit hour is sunrise. A fixed :40 cannot
   follow sunrise, which moves by hours over the year, so this is a
   separate reading rather than an explanation of :40 (see the sunrise
   conjecture below).
4. **The ger as a direction dial.** The light patch from the toono moves
   around the ger, and sayings tie its positions to hours. This gives
   direction-based hours, not clock times (see [light.md](light.md)).

Reading of the table:

- In Ulaanbaatar, in the centres east of about 104°E, and in the three
  western centres on UTC+7, mean noon is within about 25 minutes of 12:40.
  There the light points north close to the middle of Horse hour all year.
- The western centres on UTC+8 (Altai, Uliastai, Mörön, Bayankhongor,
  Tsetserleg) have noon 35–55 minutes late, so the light reaches north in
  the second half of Horse hour or, at Altai and Uliastai in winter, after
  it.
- **Lhasa** keeps Beijing time at 91°E. Its mean noon is at 13:55. On
  14 November apparent noon falls almost exactly at 13:40, so the light
  points north at the very end of Horse hour. This is what the tool showed
  and what prompted the comparison. At other times of year north is
  reached only after Horse hour ends.

The tool's «Жинхэнэ нарны» reading uses apparent solar time and so always
centres Horse on true noon, wherever the place and whatever the zone.

## The four readings in the tool

1. **:40 clock** (`clockHour`): the fixed convention in the selected time
   zone.
2. **True solar** (`solarHour`): equal double hours with Horse centred on
   apparent noon.
3. **Ger direction** (`directionHour`): twelve fixed 30° sectors of the
   ger, turned with the door. The hour is the sector the light falls in. The
   light lies opposite the Sun's azimuth, so this reads the Sun's azimuth.
   The Sun's azimuth does not change at a steady rate, so these hours are
   unequal and change with the season. In summer the light crosses the
   sectors around noon quickly and the morning and evening sectors slowly.
4. **From sunrise** (`temporalHour`): daylight split into six equal parts,
   Rabbit beginning at sunrise. Night is split the same way.

## The sunrise conjecture

The author's conjecture: since the traditional day begins at sunrise,
Rabbit hour may by definition begin at sunrise, and fixing hours to noon or
midnight is a recent practice in Mongolia.

Consequences that can be checked in the tool:

- With six equal daylight parts starting at sunrise, noon falls at the
  boundary between Snake and Horse, not in the middle of Horse.
- Equal hours with Horse centred on noon put Rabbit at 05:00–07:00 apparent
  time. At the equinox the Sun rises at about 06:00 apparent time (a few
  minutes earlier with refraction), which is the middle of Rabbit, not its
  start. If Rabbit is tied to the first light of dawn rather than sunrise,
  the two readings move closer together.
- Whether the sayings mean dawn or sunrise, and whether Horse is centred on
  noon or begins at it, is open.

## Sayings about the light

The sayings collected during the work tie the light in the ger to hours:

- light on the toono: Tiger–Rabbit,
- from the uni heads (at the toono) down to the wall head: Dragon–Snake,
- on the khoimor (the place of honour opposite the door): Horse, at noon.

A 2010 post on the Ари-Мгл blog (see sources) gives a
similar sequence in clock hours: light on the toono in the early morning
(about 4–6), on the uni at about 6–10, and noon at 12. It also says each uni
hole is named after one of the twelve animals. It names the small
four-khana ger with 60 uni as the one that matches the sun clock exactly
(see [ger-geometry.md](ger-geometry.md)). The light reaches the toono
ring and the upper uni only when the Sun is low, so these sayings fit
early-morning and evening hours. Around the winter solstice the noon Sun in Ulaanbaatar is only about 19°
high, so even at noon the patch is on the wall and the lower uni rather than
on the floor.
